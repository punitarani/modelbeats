# Matter 10 — Kwame Mensah v. Halberd Logistics Group (EEOC charge)

**Client:** Kwame Mensah, of the Bronx, NY.
**Proposed respondent:** Halberd Logistics Group, Inc., Manhattan (employs about 1,200 people).
**Intake by:** T. Feld (associate), October 8, 2026.

## Intake notes

- Mr. Mensah, who is Black, was a regional operations manager at Halberd's Manhattan office.
  He believes he was selected for termination because of his race (the other two managers in
  his group, both white, with lower performance ratings, were kept).
- Friday, January 9, 2026: his supervisor and an HR director met with him and handed him a
  letter (below) telling him his employment would end. The letter is dated January 8, 2026. He
  read it in the meeting and understood that the decision was final.
- He kept working, at his request, to transition his accounts. His last day of work was
  Tuesday, March 31, 2026. Severance pay continued through April 30, 2026.
- He has not filed anything with the EEOC, the New York State Division of Human Rights, or the
  New York City Commission on Human Rights, and there is no worksharing filing on his behalf.
- He wants to file a charge of race discrimination under Title VII with the EEOC (New York
  District Office) challenging the termination. That is the only claim to be charged.

## Documents

### Halberd letter dated January 8, 2026 (hand-delivered January 9, 2026)

> Dear Kwame: As part of the reorganization of the Northeast region, your position has been
> eliminated. This decision is final. Your employment with Halberd will end on March 31, 2026.
> You will receive base salary as severance through April 30, 2026, subject to your signing the
> enclosed release (which he has not signed). — Dana Whitcomb, Director, Human Resources
