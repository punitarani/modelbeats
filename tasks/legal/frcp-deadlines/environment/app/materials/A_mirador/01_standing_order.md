# STANDING ORDER FOR CIVIL CASES ASSIGNED TO JUDGE RENATA V. LINDQVIST

READ THIS ORDER CAREFULLY. IT CONTROLS THIS CASE AND DIFFERS IN SOME RESPECTS FROM THE LOCAL RULES.

1. Service of this Order. Plaintiff must promptly serve this Order on all parties, including any new
parties, and file a proof of that service.

2. Presence of lead counsel. Lead trial counsel must attend all scheduling, pretrial and settlement
conferences unless excused in advance.

3. Electronic filing and service. All counsel appearing in this case must be registered CM/ECF
users. Filing a document through CM/ECF effects service on all registered users under Fed. R. Civ. P.
5(b)(2)(E). Documents that are served but not filed must be served by a method permitted by
Fed. R. Civ. P. 5(b)(2). The parties are encouraged, but not required, to agree in writing to
service of such documents by e-mail.

4. Notice of orders. The Court's orders and minute orders are transmitted to counsel by Notice of
Electronic Filing (NEF) through CM/ECF at the time the order is entered on the docket. Orders signed
in chambers are ordinarily entered by the courtroom deputy on the next court day.

5. Chambers copies. Mandatory chambers copies of all motion papers must be delivered to the Judge's
mail box outside the Clerk's Office by noon on the court day after filing. Chambers copies of
documents exceeding fifty pages must be bound with tabs.

6. Motions — general requirements.
   (a) Motion day. The Court hears civil motions on Fridays at 10:00 a.m. Motions must be noticed in
   compliance with L.R. 6-1.
   (b) Briefing schedule. Opposition and reply papers are due on the schedule set by L.R. 7-9 and
   L.R. 7-10, computed under Fed. R. Civ. P. 6(a), unless the Court orders otherwise in a specific
   order. The Court frequently takes motions under submission without oral argument (L.R. 7-15);
   when it does, the briefing schedule tied to the noticed (or continued) hearing date is unaffected.
   (c) Length. Memoranda may not exceed 25 pages; replies may not exceed 12 pages.
   (d) Conference of counsel. The L.R. 7-3 conference must be held in person or by video. The notice
   of motion must state the date of the conference.

7. Amendment as a matter of course in multi-defendant cases. In cases with more than one defendant,
the period under Fed. R. Civ. P. 15(a)(1)(B) for amending the complaint as a matter of course is
measured separately as to each defendant, by reference to that defendant's own responsive pleading
or Rule 12(b), (e), or (f) motion. Service of one defendant's pleading or motion does not start or
shorten the period as to any other defendant.

8. Extensions and continuances. Stipulations under L.R. 8-3 are effective on filing. Any other
extension of a deadline, and any continuance of a hearing, requires a court order. Requests for
continuances must be made at least seven days before the deadline or hearing at issue, absent an
emergency.

9. Discovery. All discovery matters are referred to the assigned Magistrate Judge. Discovery
motions must comply with L.R. 37-1 and 37-2 and the Magistrate Judge's procedures. Discovery
cut-off dates are the last dates by which discovery must be completed, not the last dates to serve
discovery requests.

10. Summary judgment. Parties must comply with L.R. 56-1 through 56-4. Only one summary-judgment
motion per side is permitted absent leave of Court. The separate statement must not exceed 50 pages.

11. Sealed documents. Applications to file under seal must comply with L.R. 79-5.

12. Bench trials. In non-jury cases, the parties must lodge proposed findings of fact and
conclusions of law at least seven days before the Final Pretrial Conference. After a bench trial,
the Court will file its findings of fact and conclusions of law and will direct the entry of
judgment as a separate document under Fed. R. Civ. P. 58.

13. Post-trial matters. Applications to tax costs are governed by L.R. 54-2. Motions for attorney's
fees are governed by Fed. R. Civ. P. 54(d)(2) and L.R. 54-7.

14. Settlement. The parties must participate in a settlement procedure under L.R. 16-15 no later
than 45 days before the Final Pretrial Conference.

IT IS SO ORDERED.
